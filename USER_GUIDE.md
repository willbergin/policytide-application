# Policy Tide user guide

[Project overview](README.md)

Policy Tide helps you explore selected UK policy changes, compare parties' 2024 election promises and estimate how certain tax and pay scenarios could affect your take-home pay. It is an independent, curated prototype, not live news, voting advice or a complete financial assessment.

## Get started

Open the application. You can browse without an account or a profile. Use the main navigation: **Overview**, **Updates**, **Promises** and **Finances**. The page headings use the fuller names **What's changing**, **Party promises** and **Your finances**.

Select **Your profile** to choose the topics you care about and, if you wish, your location, housing, work and family circumstances. Save your choices to bring relevant items towards the top. The app explains the matched interests or circumstances behind a recommendation. It does not assign a political leaning or tell you how to vote.

Your profile is stored in this browser, not in a user account. You can edit or clear it at any time. Clearing browser data can also remove it.

## Overview

The Overview highlights selected developments and promises, with a featured update and a short set of records. It is a starting point rather than a complete news feed. Personalisation changes the order, not the underlying political judgements.

## What's changing

Browse curated policy developments, each with a date, source and stage such as **Announced**, **Planned change**, **Implementation** or **In force**. Use the topic filter to focus on an area.

Check which parts of the UK a change applies to. The app can prioritise entries relevant to your selected nation, but you should still read the jurisdiction and source before acting. An announcement does not automatically mean a change is already law or in effect.

## Party promises

You can explore promises in three ways:

- **Relevant to you** prioritises selected topics and circumstances.
- **Explore promises** lets you search or filter by party, topic and status. Eight results are shown initially; choose **Show more promises** to see more.
- **Compare parties** places two parties' **2024 manifesto proposals** alongside one another for a chosen policy topic.

The Labour tracker contains **32 selected 2024 commitments** with dated source-linked assessments, evidence explanations and timelines. Open **Promise details and evidence** to see how a judgement was reached and follow its links.

As of 21 November 2026, all 32 have an assessment. Six were reviewed earlier; 26 were published following AI-assisted source research requested by the owner and were **not separately fact-checked by an independent human editor**. Treat the labels as dated editorial assessments and review the underlying evidence.

**Delivered**, **In progress**, **On track**, **Off track** and **Not delivered** describe the assessment of a commitment, not whether you should support it. **Unclear** means the evidence or measurement does not justify a stronger conclusion. For any future unreviewed entries, **Awaiting assessment** means no judgement has been made.

Records for the Conservatives, Liberal Democrats, Reform UK, Greens, SNP and Plaid Cymru are **historical 2024 manifesto positions**. They are not necessarily those parties' current policies and are not given delivery verdicts.

## Your finances

Open **Finances** and enter your annual employment salary and optional projected percentage pay change. Depending on the scenario, select your UK nation in the profile, an optional **salary sacrifice pension** percentage, one undergraduate student loan plan (**1, 2, 4 or 5**) and an optional postgraduate loan. Select **Update estimate**.

The app compares estimated annual employment income tax, employee National Insurance and take-home pay for **2026/27 and 2027/28**, including an average monthly equivalent. It uses annualised rules rather than your employer's exact payroll rounding. A salary increase you enter is your own scenario and is not automatically a Budget benefit.

This is **not** a complete household or tax calculator. It does not model all income, benefits, pension methods or deductions. The pension option is specifically **salary sacrifice**, not net-pay or relief-at-source contributions. The Plan 4 student loan threshold for 2027/28 is provisional, and Scottish employment income tax for that year is unsupported. Wales is provisionally modelled using the main UK rates. Check the assumptions and source links shown with the results.

## How personalisation and privacy work

Policy Tide uses the interests and circumstances you select to rank related material. It does not hide all other parties or infer your voting intentions. A closer match does not imply that a policy is good for you, that it will happen or that you should support the party behind it.

Your answers are kept in local browser storage. There is no login, cross-device sync or app analytics. If you clear the profile or browser site data, the saved choices may disappear.

## When information looks wrong or out of date

Start with the original source linked to the record and check both its publication date and the record's review date. Tax rules, bills and government announcements can change after a snapshot was prepared. The monitoring tools generate research leads for review; they do not automatically revise what you read in the app.

Policy Tide is a prototype for understanding public information, not a replacement for official legal, tax or financial advice.
